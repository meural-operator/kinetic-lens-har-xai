# What the KARD concept results actually explain

The strongest defensible conclusion is that the frozen representation contains directions that distinguish groups of action labels, and the trained KARD head responds to those directions. Their human semantic meaning is still only a hypothesis. Validation status remains **PARTIAL**.

## Separation asks whether information is available

Imagine every video becoming 480 numbers. A probe asks whether one weighted sum of those numbers places selected actions above the others. AUC 0.985 for waving means a randomly chosen validation waving clip scores above a randomly chosen negative clip in about 98.5% of their pairs. It does not mean 98.5% of videos are classified correctly,98.5% of waving is explained, or 98.5% of a prediction is caused by arms. At the original zero cutoff, waving sensitivity is only 2/3, despite excellent ranking. Balanced accuracy gives equal weight to positive and negative detection, avoiding the large negative class dominating ordinary accuracy.

The labels here say that Horizontal arm wave, High arm wave and Two hand wave belong to a family. They do not independently say which joints moved or how much. The probe can exploit anything correlated with those actions: motion trajectory, orientation, held items, recording context or combinations. These are alternative explanations that current data cannot separate. Subject holdout reduces exact person memorization but does not remove action-correlated nuisance cues. The backbone was pretrained for generic action recognition and frozen on KARD; only the final HAR classifier learned KARD. The auxiliary probe's accuracy does not demonstrate that the HAR head actually uses its chosen separating direction.

The old shuffled-label controls established association beyond random clip labels. They did not turn action-derived labels into independent semantics. In fact, assigning a constant family target to each action makes within-action feature/target covariance zero after action-centering. The extended audit measures this as 8.39e-17. No annotation tells the probe that two clips of the same action differ in object visibility, posture or leg motion. That missing variation is the central reason semantic identification remains incomplete.

## Transfer asks whether the rule extends beyond named actions

Exclude Horizontal arm wave from probe coefficient fitting and calibration. Then test its six videos against negative actions from new subjects. The alpha 10 AUC falls from full-family 0.985 to 0.650, and remains 0.574-0.793 under every fixed penalty studied. A direction that separates known waving actions poorly ranks another waving action. It might encode a narrower motion or pose pattern; we have no independent labels to choose that explanation over action-specific cues.

Exclude Drink from object-interaction training and the alpha 10 AUC is 0.743. It ranges 0.731-0.965 across penalties. This is evidence that the earlier transfer failure depends on regularization; it is not grounds to choose the successful setting after seeing validation. The whole range is reported. Kicking and posture transfer have consistently high ranking (minimum 0.9965/0.9944), giving better action-family transfer evidence in this cohort, while leaving semantic confounds unresolved.

Some failures are about cutoff as well as ranking. Bend AUC is 1 at every penalty, but its six held-out positives all lie below zero. They are correctly ordered against negatives, yet an unsuitable numerical cutoff misses every one. Train-only subject-fold calibration at alpha 10 recognizes three, bringing BA from 0.500 to 0.750. Other fixed penalties still miss all six with their independently calibrated cutoffs. Good ordering does not promise a transferable decision boundary. We calibrated thresholds, not probabilities, and did not alter the HAR model.

The validation subjects were previously used to select the original HAR head and to assess the pilot. They are held out from these probe fits, but cannot be presented as a pristine confirmatory cohort. Each omitted action has six clips from only two people. A single different take changes sensitivity by 1/6. The study therefore reports descriptive patterns, not statistical claims about all people.

## A concept direction is a choice, not a unique semantic axis

A probe coefficient points in the direction that increases its score. It is a classification filter; it need not trace the visual attribute itself. Training subject block resamples retain good family ranking while giving median direction cosines 0.716-0.789 relative to the full-training fit. One object-interaction draw falls to 0.461. Directions can differ substantially while making similar ordering decisions. Pahde et al. explain how a separating filter can be influenced by distracting correlated coordinates ([ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/852870d56e20a22fcd52ea14523fb820-Abstract-Conference.html)); that methodological possibility is consistent with our measured direction variability, but does not identify a particular KARD distractor.

Our directions overlap more with training action-centroid geometry than ambient random directions. Their head-family contrast cosines are positive but modest. That tells us the representation's action organization, the probe's weighting and the HAR readout partly agree. It does not decide whether the agreement is based on useful semantics or correlated action identity. The different proxy directions also overlap and are not a complete orthogonal vocabulary of the model.

## TCAV at this endpoint cannot tell clips apart

TCAV uses directional derivatives to examine concept sensitivity ([Kim et al., ICML 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/kim18d.html)). Here the endpoint is immediately before a linear dense classifier. After saved standardization the target logit is a weighted sum, ell_c=z^T W_c+b_c. Its derivative along v is W_c^T v. There is no z in that expression: the same number applies to every video.

Consequently a TCAV fraction of 1 means that increasing this coordinate direction raises that class logit for every feature vector, because of the head's fixed geometry. A fraction 0 means its slope is nonpositive. It cannot reveal that one Drink clip relies on an object while another relies on hand motion. It is not evidence of universal concept dependence. This is an exact mathematical limitation of this endpoint, not a failure of autodiff or evidence against TCAV at all nonlinear layers.

Probe intercepts further matter: the score uses z^T coefficient plus an intercept, whereas our deletion zeros the train-mean-anchored coordinate z^T v. A threshold shift changes the proxy detector's boundary but does not change the deletion origin. Changing feature scaling or the intervention anchor can also change results. All coordinates and anchors are fixed and documented here.

## Deletion asks what the head does after a feature intervention

Deletion subtracts the vector projection on the unit direction: z'=z-(z^T v)v. The exact target-logit change is -(z^T v)(W_c^T v). It depends on both how much projection the clip has and how the head aligns with that direction. A direction can be strongly separable yet weakly aligned with a particular head column. Conversely any head-aligned direction can change a prediction without corresponding to a readable human concept.

We extend the old three examples to all 108 validation clips. Waving, kicking, object interaction and posture deletions change 1/0/4/1 predictions respectively. The true-class readout is always measured, even on the 12 baseline mistakes. A changed class can correct a previous mistake; CSVs report net accuracy changes and both predictions. Comparing true-class probabilities alone would hide movements in competitors, so we also provide margins and logits.

Among family-positive clips, mean true-class logit drops are 0.397/0.708/0.129/0.587. Equal-L 2 ambient random displacement means are 0.025/0.049/0.013/0.043. This supports directional head sensitivity beyond arbitrary ambient displacement. However random displacements within the action-centroid span give 0.180/0.372/0.097/0.308, substantially closer. Radial shrinkage can give even larger drops. Thus a meaningful action-readout direction can outperform ambient randomness without identifying a unique semantic mechanism. The random controls have the same per-clip perturbation length; otherwise a direction could appear more important merely because it removed more feature energy.

No physically edited video is involved. The new feature vector may correspond to no realizable video at all, or to changes in several attributes. Zeroing one dimension need not remove a semantic property represented redundantly in many dimensions. Unchanged prediction therefore does not prove the concept was unused, and a changed prediction does not prove a human concept was causally removed. Logit drop is a faithful explanation of this specified linear representation intervention; semantic faithfulness remains unverified.

## What would change the conclusion

Obtain independent annotations with variation inside action classes and overlap across them: visible held object, arm above shoulder, knee extension, torso flexion or similarly observable properties. Document definitions, rater agreement, counterexamples and uncertain labels. Annotators should not inspect model scores. Train and assess with whole subjects/actions held out, and include cases where action membership disagrees with the attribute. Then compare direction estimates, nonlinear-layer sensitivity, class/head controls and realistic video edits. This would let us test whether a direction tracks the attribute rather than merely the action label. Until then, the correct statement is action-family probe association and deployed-head directional sensitivity, with partial semantic validation.
