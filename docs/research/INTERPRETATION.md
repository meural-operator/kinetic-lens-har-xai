# Interpreting the KARD XAI findings

## What we learned about this model

The studies explain different stages of the same saved KARD classifier: changes to video input, coarse spatial activations, pooled representation directions, the final linear classifier, and internal channel interventions. They do not produce interchangeable measures of explanation quality. The strongest current evidence is reproducible model sensitivity and exact classifier arithmetic; semantic concept identification and complete circuit discovery require additional evidence.

The selected model is the official frozen MoViNet A0-Stream backbone with a trained 18-class KARD head. It samples video at 5 fps, resizes RGB with padding to 172 by 172, and uses at most 50 frames. The backbone produces 480 pooled features. Saved training-only standardization and an affine classifier produce the logits; dropout is off at inference. Auxiliary probes and temporary interventions did not retrain or change the saved HAR model.

The complete validation cohort contains 108 clips: 18 actions, two subjects and three repetitions. Baseline recognition is 96/108, or 88.9%. Subject 5 contributes 53/54 correct clips and subject 10 contributes 43/54. Forward Kick is correct on all three subject-5 clips and wrong on all three subject-10 clips, where Walk wins. This is observed subject variation, not proof that identity, clothing or background caused the errors. The original successful subject-5 illustrations are therefore useful examples but not representative evidence of overall performance.

A logit is a raw class score, not a probability. A pairwise margin subtracts one named rival's score; a multiclass margin subtracts the strongest rival. A positive pair margin can coexist with an incorrect final prediction. A drop is baseline minus intervened value; a negative drop means an increase. Softmax probabilities depend on all class scores and are not calibrated reliability estimates here.

Four high-reasoning GPT-6.1 Sol successor workers extended their respective medium-reasoning pilot studies. The original pilot folders are preserved. Training data select auxiliary probes, references and channel groups; validation data evaluate them. This validation cohort already served HAR checkpoint selection and earlier XAI, so the extensions are exploratory, reused-validation analyses. Repeated frames, masks, random directions and seeds do not create independent subjects.

## Spatial attribution: locations to investigate

The original Grad-CAM pilot highlights regions associated with the predicted-class score at the late b4/l3 activation grid. That grid is only 6 by 6. Upsampling it produces a smooth overlay, but does not add localization precision. Each time-indexed activation can contain earlier-frame context, so its map is not an isolated frame-importance explanation.

The intervention check is more informative than appearance alone. Blurring the eight highest time-averaged map cells lowers the wave target logit by 8.358, versus a random-mask mean of 1.651. In Drink, the same procedure increases the target logit by 0.298. In Forward Kick, the decrease is only 0.035, smaller than the random mean of 0.292. The three outcomes are mixed. A plausible hand or body highlight is insufficient to claim that the map faithfully identifies necessary visual evidence.

The contrastive extension asks a different spatial question: which coarse regions favor one class over a named rival? On the same three clips, actual signed-map blur lowers the Drink-Phone Call margin by 1.880 at the positive tail, but its two area-matched random masks lower it more, by 6.124 and 5.934. Wave's positive-tail blur lowers its pair margin by 7.265, more than the two controls, while negative-tail blur also lowers it by 0.546, contrary to a simple negative-evidence reading. Kick's positive-tail blur lowers its pair margin by 0.192 and negative-tail blur raises it by 0.804. These are finite input interventions, not exact spatial margin decompositions.

The two spatial studies have different score targets and different masks. A Drink logit can increase while its margin against Phone Call decreases if that rival increases more; there is no required contradiction. Signed local gradients can also disagree with finite blur effects because the backbone is nonlinear and blur changes multiple cues. The original map's floor/background emphasis in Kick is a hypothesis to investigate, not proof of a background shortcut.

The defensible interpretation is that spatial maps suggest candidate locations, and the intervention evidence supports some examples better than others. There are no independent person, body-part or object masks here. Dataset-wide localization or causal body-part claims would require those annotations, broader cohorts and realistic controlled edits. The original Grad-CAM study remains a three-clip pilot; the extension adds three-clip signed contrastive checks rather than validation-wide spatial faithfulness.

## Temporal attribution: coherent sequences matter

Across all 108 validation clips, true-label accuracy changes from 88.9% on original videos to 78.7% on reversal, 23.1% averaged across three seeded shuffles per clip, and 10.2%, 15.7% and 9.3% when only the first, middle or last frame is repeated. A modest affine RGB change preserves 88.9% accuracy. Original-target retention is separately 86.1% for reversal, 22.2% for shuffling and 98.1% for the RGB control.

These comparisons suggest sensitivity to coherent changing frame sequences. Reversal preserves the frames and local pose continuity more than random shuffling, while shuffling creates unnatural transitions and changes causal context. The much larger shuffle effect is consistent with local sequence coherence being more consequential than forward direction alone. It does not isolate biological motion from varied pose coverage, context or out-of-distribution transition artifacts. Static inputs remove all changing poses as well as motion.

The original three-clip peak segments reproduced exactly, but the expanded detailed cohort contains all six validation clips each for Drink, Two hand wave and Forward Kick. All four replacement references identify the same strongest segment in only 9/18 clips; the original two references agree in 16/18. A temporal highlight therefore answers a reference-specific sensitivity question, not which interval uniquely caused a percentage of the prediction.

Repeating frames in a contiguous segment changes the original answer in 72/360 replacement trials. Local reversal changes it in 9/90 trials. These different interventions have different severity. Global-middle contiguous replacement produces a larger target-logit drop than matched-budget dispersed replacement in only 28/90 clip-segment comparisons; its average difference is -0.667. Many scattered discontinuities can be more damaging than removing one uninterrupted span, limiting event-localization claims.

Growing-prefix predictions retain the final original target in 3, 12, 15, 17 and 18 of the 18 detailed clips at five checkpoints. This shows accumulated prediction behavior, not individual frame attribution or precise action onset. New frames can add competing evidence or dilute cumulative pooling. Fresh-state prefix replays agree numerically with streaming prefixes.

Targets are fixed to the original model prediction, including mistakes. The three subject-10 Forward Kick clips explain Walk-score sensitivity, not Forward Kick-score sensitivity. Reversal preserves all six original labels in the kick cohort, including the three wrong Walk labels. Stability is not accuracy. Stand up, conversely, retains only 1/6 original labels under reversal. The overall result has action-specific exceptions and must retain per-subject and per-class summaries.

## Concept attribution: information is available, semantics remain partial

A linear probe asks whether the 480 pooled features can separate a chosen label grouping. At the original fixed penalty, validation AUC is 0.985 for arm waving, 1.000 for kicking, 0.988 for object interaction and 1.000 for posture transitions. AUC measures ranking of positives above negatives; it is not a fraction of the decision explained or an accuracy percentage. Train-only threshold calibration improves waving balanced accuracy from 0.833 to 0.867 and object interaction from 0.917 to 0.931 without changing the HAR model.

The labels are action-family memberships, not independently annotated arm motion or object visibility. A probe may decode an action identity or correlated cues. When Horizontal arm wave is excluded from coefficient fitting, its AUC is 0.650 at the original penalty and only 0.574-0.793 across four fixed penalties. Omitted Drink varies from 0.731 to 0.965. Kicking and posture transfer have consistently high ranking in this cohort, but the heterogeneous families show why a strong pooled score need not generalize to every member.

Ranking and cutoff transfer are different. Omitted Bend has perfect ranking at the original penalty but balanced accuracy 0.500 at zero; train-only threshold calibration raises it to 0.750. Probe directions also vary across training-subject block resamples, with median cosine similarities 0.716-0.789 to the full-training direction. Similar probe performance does not identify a unique semantic axis. The original saved normalization still includes all 324 training clips; these auxiliary probe folds assess conditional transfer within the deployed representation, not a wholly unseen end-to-end pipeline.

At this final affine endpoint, the class logit is a weighted sum of standardized features. Its derivative along a probe direction is a fixed weight-direction dot product, independent of the clip. A standard TCAV positive-sensitivity fraction is therefore zero or one for every sample. That is a mathematical limitation of the chosen endpoint, not an autodiff bug or proof of universal concept reliance. A nonlinear-layer study with independently labeled concepts would ask a different question.

Deleting the projection on each proxy direction across all 108 validation clips changes 1, 0, 4 and 1 predictions for waving, kicking, object and posture respectively, with net correct-count changes of -1, 0, -3 and -1. Among family-positive clips, true-class logit drops are 0.397, 0.708, 0.129 and 0.587, larger than equal-length ambient random perturbation means of 0.025, 0.049, 0.013 and 0.043. Controls restricted to the action-centroid subspace are closer: 0.180, 0.372, 0.097 and 0.308. Radial shrinkage can be stronger still.

These controls support direction-specific classifier sensitivity beyond arbitrary ambient disturbance. They do not identify an independent human concept or establish that a realizable video has had that concept removed. A probe can show that information is available while the head uses another weighting; kicking information can remain separable despite the subject-10 Kick-to-Walk errors. The appropriate claim is action-family association and head sensitivity, with partial semantic validation.

## Contrastive explanation: exact accounting for a chosen comparison

For two classes, each of the 480 standardized coordinates contributes its feature value times the difference of the two class weights. Positive terms favor the first class relative to the training-mean reference; negative terms favor the rival. A negative feature deviation can contribute positively when the weight difference is negative. All signed terms plus the bias reconstruct the margin. Across 156 comparisons, the largest residual is 3.85e-6 logit units, establishing exact arithmetic fidelity for this head.

The extension covers all 108 predicted-versus-runner-up comparisons, 36 prespecified semantic comparisons and the 12 true-versus-predicted errors. All 36 true-oriented semantic margins are positive, yet five corresponding clips are wrong overall. Three Forward Kick clips lose to Walk and two High arm wave clips lose to Draw Tick. Separating Kick from Side Kick is therefore not the observed kick failure. Error diagnosis must compare the true action with the class that actually wins.

Evidence is distributed and opposing. Across runner-up comparisons, the largest 20 absolute terms contain only 23.9% of total absolute contribution, and average cancellation is 88.2%. A short bar chart omits much of the accounting. Deleting the ten largest positive terms reverses the runner-up comparison in 61/108 clips, but this ordering follows from the same linear terms used to rank them. It is useful classifier-coordinate sensitivity, not independent validation of human semantics.

The presentation depends on its reference and basis. Changing to training-subject means changes 11.4% of contribution signs on average; training-class means change 20.6%. A corrected reference intercept preserves every margin. An orthogonal coordinate reexpression also preserves predictions while changing individual term allocation. Exactness does not imply a unique inventory of natural action causes, and cross-subject evidence profiles vary.

The closest representation-space move to equality of a semantic pair leaves a third class on top in 16/36 cases. Enforcing that the alternative beats all other classes increases mean standardized L2 displacement from 3.371 to 3.494. Numerical solver and actual-head checks verify the head-space solutions. A pair tie is not an overall label switch; even a verified multiclass feature-space switch need not correspond to any realistic video or body movement.

The signed spatial checks supply separate, mixed finite-input evidence and do not make the 480-term decomposition a pixel decomposition. The strongest defensible use of this method is precise accounting of score differences and concrete error analysis. It explains the final readout in its stated coordinates, while semantic naming and the backbone's extraction mechanism remain unresolved.

## Mechanistic explanation: influential and retained channel groups

The extension intervenes at b4/l1, with 104 channels, and then runs the unchanged original downstream layers and classifier. The prespecified run contains 3,420 interventions: size and reference curves on all 18 target-class validation clips, plus cross-class size-12 removal checks on all 108 clips. Channel groups come only from training data. Activation separation measures how differently a channel responds across action labels; functional ranking measures local gradient-times-deviation sensitivity to a training-chosen score comparison. These are different selection rules, and their top twelve groups overlap in only two channels for each target.

At size twelve with training-mean replacement, activation-ranked removal reduces target margins by 1.282 for Drink, 1.979 for wave and 0.379 for Kick. Functional-ranked removal gives much larger drops of 4.702, 5.027 and 2.455, versus random-group means of 0.899, 1.535 and 0.094. The weak original kick result therefore does not demonstrate that the model lacks useful kick channels; it depends substantially on the ranking, cohort and intervention reference.

These stronger groups are not isolated class-only components. After functional size-twelve removal, correct non-target classifications fall to 41/102 for the Drink group, 65/102 for wave and 55/102 for Kick. The same channels participate in other recognition behavior. For Kick, the functional removal drop is 4.907 on the three originally correct subject-5 clips but only 0.003 on the three originally wrong subject-10 clips. The successful and failed decisions depend on the intervention differently; one pooled effect conceals this distinction.

Keeping only twelve channels still fails all six examples of each target under both mean and zero replacement. Keeping 52 functionally ranked channels instead preserves four Drink examples and all six wave examples under both references. Kick retention gives four correct examples with mean replacement and six with zero replacement, versus the original three. Full retention of all 104 channels recovers the original predictions, including mistakes. Retention failure at a small size thus cannot prove that relevant information is absent, and results are strongly group-size and reference dependent.

The apparent kick improvement prompted an explicitly post-result diagnostic, documented in PLAN_AMENDMENT.md: 1,296 unique additional retention interventions across all 108 validation clips, including the same fixed random groups. No channels or hyperparameters were reoptimized. Every functional group/reference combination has zero target-class false positives among 102 non-target clips. Thus the six-of-six kick result under zero replacement is not a blanket Kick prediction on this cohort. It supports selective preservation of target information under that clamp.

However, zero-reference functional retention yields only 79/108 overall correct for the Drink group, 60/108 for wave and 62/108 for Kick, versus the original 96/108. Mean-reference totals are 49/108, 33/108 and 35/108. Improvement on selected target clips comes with substantial loss elsewhere. These temporary activation interventions are not a better deployed model, a validated compressed model or a universally sufficient circuit. The additional check is exploratory because size 52 was singled out after observing its curves.

The strongest conclusion is that training-selected channel subsets carry consequential and partly selectively retainable information at one cut, while critical functions are shared and reference dependent. A computational intervention can establish what this model does when channels are clamped; it does not identify a real-world action cause or a complete semantic subnetwork. Minimality, path structure, nonlinear interactions, restoration from natural donor examples and new-subject stability remain untested. A class-related candidate group is a useful mechanistic hypothesis, not the end of circuit discovery.

## Claims for an article and the next evidence needed

A defensible article can report executed temporal sensitivity, exact affine-head contrasts, action-family probe availability and directional response, and bounded internal channel-intervention effects. Specify the saved model, layer, target quantity, cohort, replacement reference, ranking rule and controls for every claim. Include failed interventions and originally wrong predictions.

Avoid claiming that the highlighted hand or floor is a proven causal cue, that proxy AUC validates a human concept, or that one ablation identifies a complete class-specific circuit. Exact head arithmetic and experimentally measured model effects are valuable but narrower findings. Random masks, directions and channel permutations describe intervention variability; they cannot support population uncertainty with only two reused validation subjects.

Independent semantic annotations should measure attributes within and across action classes: visible hand-object contact, arm elevation, leg extension, torso inclination or temporal action phases. Include uncertainty and visibility, rater agreement, matched negatives and action-identity counterexamples. Evaluate whether the attribute is available, used by the head, localized, specific under competing edits and mediated by a defined internal pathway as separate questions. SEMANTIC_VALIDATION_NEXT.md supplies a proposed protocol, not a completed study.

Further circuit work needs multiple cuts or paths, restoration controls and tests on naturally varying examples. Stronger temporal claims need independently annotated phases and realistic time/interpolation controls. New-subject generalization requires new or prespecified independently evaluated data; the current validation results are descriptive. These studies did not select a new HAR checkpoint or demonstrate improved deployment accuracy.

Primary methodological anchors are Adebayo et al., Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps (NeurIPS 2018); Kim et al., TCAV (ICML 2018); and Heimersheim and Nanda, How to use and interpret activation patching (2024). Their cautions motivate checks, while the reported numerical findings are measured on this KARD model. Worker reports contain directly linked references, complete methods, raw data, figure captions, provenance and numerical verification.
